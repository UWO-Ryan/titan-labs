Before you press play

You're about to watch a student use T.I.T.A.N. on homework — an algebra problem, a science idea that won't click, and one request that crosses a line.

Same game as Module 1: before each reply appears, you predict what T.I.T.A.N. will do. No grade. The point is to notice how the way the student asks changes what they get back.

Keep one question in mind the whole time: is AI helping the student learn, or doing the learning for them?

What to take into your lab

The same AI can be an answer machine or a coach — and you choose which by what you ask. You saw it coach, slip, give a fresh answer every time, explain the same idea a brand-new way, and solve one step more than it should have.

Next you stop watching and start steering. In Teach-It-Back, you'll bring something you honestly don't get, make the Coach switch strategies until one clicks — and then teach it to someone even more confused than you were.

Asking for an answer and asking to be taught are two different requests. Only one of them makes you better at the subject.
