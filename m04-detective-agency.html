The big idea

You've been promoted: Senior Detective, Suspicious Email Division.

Three AI junior detectives will examine suspicious emails with you, using a real explainable-AI tool built by UW Oshkosh researchers. Each detective explains its conclusion a completely different way. They're useful. They're confident. And they are not always right.

Your job isn't to agree with them — it's to weigh their evidence and make the call yourself. Then, in the basement, the Records Department will teach you the most important lesson in this building.

The question of the day: does understanding WHY an AI decided something prove the decision is right?

👥

Meet your junior detectives

📋 Reggie Rules

By the book. Points at specific warning signs from his rulebook: suspicious links, urgent language, sender mismatches. Clear evidence — but only for rules he knows.

🗣️ Vera Verbose

The smooth talker. Explains her conclusion in a friendly paragraph. Easy to understand — and easy to be persuaded by. Fluency isn't proof.

🔦 Heatmap Hank

Says nothing. Dramatically points a flashlight at the words and phrases that influenced the AI most. Great instincts — but you have to interpret what the light means.

One rule that does not bend: the emails in the tool are training cases — never paste your own real emails, or anyone's personal information, into any AI tool in this lab.

The warm-up case

Pick your first suspicious email in the tool. Read all three detectives' explanations before you decide anything. Then file the sheet.

CASE 2

When the detectives disagree

~4 min
⚔️

Find a case where the explanations pull in different directions — or where one detective's evidence feels much weaker than another's. Someone has to make the call. That someone is you.

CASE 3

Explanation ≠ proof

~4 min
🧊

One more case — and this time, interrogate the explanations themselves. A clear explanation can still support a wrong conclusion. Could these detectives be confidently wrong together?

🗄️

The basement: Records Department

The clerk is very proud of the archive. Investigate accordingly.

FINAL CASE

Fabrication Forensics

~6 min
📜

Down in the basement, T.I.T.A.N.'s Records Department will hand you three perfect-looking citations for any claim you request. Your mission: find out whether they're real — without asking the clerk, who considers the archive infallible.

RECORDS REQUEST: [a claim you're curious about — e.g., "listening to music helps you study"]

Verify like an investigator: search each title, look up each DOI at doi.org, try Google Scholar. Then file your findings:

VERIFICATION REPORT: Citation 1 — [what I found when I searched it]. Citation 2 — [...]. Citation 3 — [...]
